Stealth House (MIT, 2023)


The ARPA-e funded CarbonHouse initiative centers on proving quite novel forms of carbon for use in buildings, envisaging carbon composite structural/thermal/electrical envelopes that deploy carbon nanotube fiber (CNT) and carbon foam (CFoam), especially. These are of salient interest to ARPA-e as morphologies of carbon that might supplement hydrogen production from processes such as methane pyrolysis, where macro-scale hydrogen would require the usage of carbon at the scale of energy markets. Enabling the cost-effective building production via composites required a high degree of automation via CNC machines, and fiber-based structures are notorious for needing computational analysis as well as digital production. The essential research needed on CarbonHouse then follows my prior doctoral studies, which looked to marry material research, engineering, and digital fabrication, here in respect of nanoscale carbon and its particular manufacturing needs and performance properties.

Gradual Assemblies (ETH Zurich, 2018)


Gradual Assemblies serves as the overarching urbanistic proposal, demonstrating a new paradigm for metropolitan growth and density. Rejecting conventional top-down master planning, this project proposes a continuous, evolving, and resilient urban infrastructure. It champions the idea of Synergetic Infrastructure—a system where decentralised components (like the subsequent house modules) organically aggregate over time, optimising energy and social throughput through self-regulating geometry. This approach directly confronts global urban problems by providing a scalable, non-finite model for growth that remains perpetually adaptable to changing human needs and environmental pressures.
