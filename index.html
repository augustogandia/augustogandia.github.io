Portfolio of Augusto Gandia

(November 2025 - Mendoza, Argentina)


My work explores the relation between form and force. This last one is defined by time and movement, which relies on digital means to make people move in new ways (physically and cognitively). This new latent object is referred to as an Active Inert and as Ulmer's comment suggests it enacts a requalification of creative praxis from static to dynamic environments, possibly outer space. In Derrida's terms: ‘to comprehend the structure of a becoming, the form of a force, is to lose meaning by finding it’.

Cloud Light/Light Sculptures (ETH Zurich, 2022)



These projects are the outcome of an IAP workshop conducted at MIT, titled Topographies of Light. They revolve around the desire for technology in architecture, a human driver that serves to assess a new cultural terrain. This approach moves beyond efficiency or expressivity, where the bridge to the design process is not merely a prosthesis, but a technology that infiltrates our cognitive senses. Addressing Marshall McLuhan’s concerns, these shaders and light-tracked "hallucinations" promote the release of new feelings and cultural potential, opening the mind rather than closing it. Through Sigmund Freud’s concept of the Wunderbloc (a "Mystic Writing Pad") and the "Wolfman", we are enabled to layer "active" and "passive" inscriptions, as well as to retrieve "generative"—creating vivid new ideas.

Cybermodeling (MIT, 2023)


These projects are conducted as part of a workshop at the ACADIA Conference, Habits of the Anthropocene. In contrast to Greg Lynn/FORM designs, new methods building upon those used in seminal work (Ether/I and the Shadow of Ledoux) to explore nest and shell spaces. By linking digital movement and forces with our design environment, these interactions are explored as a glimpse into new cultural praxis. In contrast to Virilio's hypothesis of data as limiting panopticism, the approach celebrates trauma as a constant suspension enabled by streams of information, inducing mental triggering or endless dis/re- orientation of new movement-performance strategies. In turn, a new world of woven, inflated, and solid constructs is unveiled as a window to cushioned and ergonomic interfaces.

Sensorium Shift (MIT, 2023)


As part of the same workshop at ACADIA, some adventurous participants extended the capture through privileged senses (eye and ear) to other channels. Besides screens, materials, sound, and touch, these projects propose to leverage light and humidity. The architectural objects were then experienced as a dance that is not so much visually (it is too complex and occluded), but as if with an extremely heightened state of cognitive awareness – a sort of bodily cognition. One could argue that the participants were ‘sniffing’ these ballets.



Assamblage: MIT and workshop participants.

Carbon House (MIT, 2021-23)


This method is developed as part of the Steath House within the context of the Carbon House. It relates to illinear creative processes, a creative practise that relates to ballet (composite panels), in the sense of a traumatic state of continual dis/re-orientation. In this now processural environment, one is not the choreographer of movement but slips into a more fluid and subtle creative paradigm of vanishing points within a generative matrix. Similar to Greg Lynn's open-ended processes, a variety of paramorphs are explored. In turn, we can mediate between extremes, capturing the latent sense of a new technical paradigm, in intellectual exchange with Peter Eisenmann. An instance of these possibilities is printed and displayed as part of the Venice Architecture Biennale.
